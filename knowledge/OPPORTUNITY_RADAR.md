# CENTRAL Opportunity Radar v1

## Purpose
CENTRAL must continuously discover, challenge, rank and test new opportunities across the whole project portfolio. The radar exists to prevent tunnel vision around one event, one sponsor, one product or one current task.

This is a McKinsey-inspired internal strategy process, not an official McKinsey framework.

## Non-negotiable brainstorming rule
Every substantive opportunity sprint has two separate phases:

1. **DIVERGE** — generate a broad opportunity universe before ranking.
2. **CONVERGE** — deduplicate, verify, score and select only the best experiments.

Do not begin with a favourite answer and then brainstorm supporting ideas.

## Eight mandatory lenses
Each broad sprint must scan all eight lenses unless the controller records why one is irrelevant:

1. **Events & rights** — events, federations, promoters, highlights, replay, media access, accreditation, result/data access.
2. **Sponsors & commercial partnerships** — cash sponsors, in-kind, affiliate, rev-share, lead-gen, employee activation, hospitality, branded content.
3. **Clubs, athletes & federations** — services, discovery, profiles, rankings, recruitment, membership, digitalisation, event support, sponsor tooling.
4. **Content, media & creators** — original formats, interviews, explainers, documentaries, social series, newsletters, podcasts, creator collaborations, syndication.
5. **Tourism, culture & community** — Luxembourg/Benelux identity, municipalities, tourism bodies, cultural institutions, schools, inclusion, volunteering, heritage/storytelling.
6. **Funding & institutional programmes** — Luxembourg, Grande Région, EU, innovation, sport, culture, youth, digitalisation and cross-border calls. Eligibility must be verified before action.
7. **Commerce & marketplace** — affiliate, equipment, tickets, merchandise, services, supplier deals, surplus, B2B packages, digital products.
8. **Technology, data & product** — PowerMap, Talent Radar, data partnerships, analytics, sponsor measurement, AI-assisted workflows, APIs, white-label/B2B tools.

## Breadth floor
Before ranking, a full portfolio sprint should target **at least 24 distinct opportunity candidates**, normally at least 3 per lens.

Portfolio diversity controls:
- no single named event/entity should account for more than 20% of candidates;
- at least half of candidates should be independent of the currently most-discussed event;
- at least one quarter should connect two or more projects or value streams where that creates real leverage;
- existing opportunities and completely new hypotheses must both be considered;
- do not create duplicate systems when an existing project/table/process can carry the opportunity.

## Candidate format
Each candidate should be one compact opportunity card:

- **Opportunity** — what could be done?
- **Customer/partner** — who gets value or pays?
- **Value at stake** — revenue, audience, strategic access, data, credibility, cost reduction or learning.
- **Why now** — deadline, event, market change, warm signal, unused asset, regulation, partner need or timing.
- **Evidence status** — VERIFIED / PARTIAL / HYPOTHESIS.
- **Smallest test** — reversible action that can generate evidence quickly.
- **Dependencies** — rights, entity status, venue, approval, technical source, budget, contact.

## Convergence score
After deduplication and evidence review, score 0–5 on:

- strategic fit
- near-term value/revenue potential
- speed to evidence
- probability of access/partner response
- reuse/leverage across PowerLux/PowerTV/MERG/Cogni
- differentiation/defensibility
- capital efficiency
- rights/legal/reputation safety

The controller may weight criteria by current company objective. A high score never overrides a hard rights, legal, privacy, security or source-truth gate.

## Execution rule
Select only **3–5 top experiments** per cycle. Each must have:

- named owner;
- one concrete deliverable;
- a decision deadline or trigger;
- measurable success/failure evidence;
- stop rule;
- owning project/value stream;
- knowledge writeback if the result changes durable state.

Do not convert the whole brainstorm into backlog clutter.

## Anti-fixation rule
A named event such as Vendetta may be a strong opportunity but is never the portfolio itself. A single event should not occupy more than one of the top five portfolio experiments unless independent verified evidence shows that multiple distinct value pools justify it.

## Source hierarchy
- Existing project/source systems first.
- Official current external sources for deadlines, programmes, events and partner facts.
- Qwen generates hypotheses, not facts.
- ChatGPT Controller verifies consequential claims before promotion to canonical knowledge or execution.

## Learning loop
Every completed experiment must answer:

1. What did we learn?
2. Did value/likelihood increase or decrease?
3. What changed in the fact base?
4. Continue, modify, scale or stop?
5. What should be added to or removed from the next opportunity universe?

The goal is **better opportunity selection and faster evidence**, not more ideas for their own sake.
